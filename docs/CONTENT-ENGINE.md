# Content That Kills — the demand engine playbook

*2026-07-08. Researched against what actually performs in the ranch/beef
niche right now. The finding that matters: **nobody owns "half cow" on
short-form** — the winners are homestead moms unboxing THEIR half cows, a
butcher channel with 1.4B views teaching cuts, and one TX ranch selling
boxes. The brand name IS the search query. Take the lane.*

## The macro gift (use in every third video)
Beef at record prices and it's national news: $9.64/lb all-fresh record,
steaks $13.02/lb (+17% YoY), ground broke $6.25/lb — and Americans bought
MORE ($45B in 2025). Costco sells a 375-lb half side for $1,900. "Ranchers
down $17B while packers profit" is circulating on TikTok. Every grocery
receipt in America is a buyhalfcow ad. React to it weekly.

## The 5 pillars (rotate; 3–4 posts/week, batch-shot)

**1. THE MATH (converts hardest — own "is half a cow worth it")**
- "here's what $1,200 of beef actually looks like" (floor covered in packs)
- "costco wants $1,900 for a half cow. let's talk about it" (react format)
- "same cut, two prices: rancher vs grocery store" (side-by-side)
- "ground beef just broke $6/lb. here's what share buyers pay"
- Remake "is buying half a cow worth it in 2026" quarterly — it's an
  evergreen search query and search compounds on YouTube Shorts.

**2. THE UNBOXING / RESTOCK (the niche's proven ASMR genre)**
- Freezer restock: hands + the thud of vacuum packs. No talking needed.
- Buyer-side UGC is the growth hack: every share buyer gets asked (review
  cron already does) — repost every buyer restock video. The customers are
  already making the best content in this genre; aggregate them.

**3. THE SYSTEM IS BROKEN (founder voice, long-burn trust)**
- "ranchers are down $17 billion while beef hits records. here's the math"
- "what the grocery store doesn't tell you about where beef comes from"
- Mission line lands here: taking back American ranching, one freezer at a
  time. Positive framing — FOR ranchers and families, never against a named
  company (brand rule).

**4. THE RANCH + THE BUILD (founder story — documented 3× engagement lever)**
- "it's 5:45am and i'm routing beef buyers to a ranch in texas" (in-the-work
  opener — solo founder building the thing IS the show)
- Rancher spotlights: 60s on the family, the land, what a share from them
  gets you → their page link
- Pickup-day / delivery-day vlogs with real families

**5. THE DROP (scarcity mechanics — only ever REAL scarcity)**
- "12 orders left from this batch" (true Orders Left numbers only)
- Shop drops, new-ranch-live announcements ("colorado, you're up")
- Farmers-market rail: "if you're in [state], you can pick this up at the
  ranch" — geo content angle nobody else can do

## Distribution hygiene (the #1 documented failure of viral farm accounts)
- Shoot vertical once → native post to TikTok + Reels + YouTube Shorts
  (Shorts titles = searchable: the half-cow queries live on YouTube).
- EVERY video: same one-line identity + link. "real beef from a ranch near
  you — buyhalfcow.com" (Parker County's system; link-in-video ≈ 40% higher
  conversion).
- $13–355 products → point at /shop (impulse ticket). Shares → point at
  /access (it's lead-gen, not checkout — the video's job is the quiz click).
- Bio → /links. Always.
- ManyChat keywords on IG comments (already planned): "BEEF" → shop link,
  "SHARE" → quiz link.

## Cadence contract (solo-founder sustainable)
3–4/week for 90 days before judging. Batch-shoot one afternoon/week: one
math video, one restock/unboxing, one founder/build clip, one react. A
predictable 3 beats a heroic 6-then-silence. Content feeds the same funnel
the machine already converts: video → shop/quiz → waitlist → routed when
supply lands.

## Why this wins (the strategic frame)
The GTM bible's two reach leaks are still the whole game: (1) content
pointed 40k at the $2k quiz instead of the $13 door — every video now
routes by ticket; (2) only ~2k of 40k owned — every video CTA feeds email/
SMS capture. Content isn't a brand project; it's the demand side of the
supply calls. A rancher on a call who's seen the videos closes himself.
